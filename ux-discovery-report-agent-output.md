# UX Discovery Report: AI Consultancy Website
## Irish Market — Discovery Phase

**Prepared for:** cillianbc.com
**Date:** March 2026
**Scope:** Full discovery across Trust, Architecture, Competition, Conversion & Technical UX

---

## Executive Summary

This report addresses a specific and narrow positioning challenge: how does a solo AI consultant in Ireland signal world-class expertise without triggering the cultural antibodies Irish business culture reserves for self-promoters and over-sellers? The five sections below move from the psychological to the architectural, from the emotional to the technical. Each section ends with concrete, implementable recommendations — not frameworks to be workshopped into oblivion.

The central thesis running through all five sections is this: **Irish B2B buyers buy people before they buy services.** The website's primary job is not to explain what GEO is. It is to make a CMO in Sandyford feel like they already know and trust the person behind the site before they ever send an email.

## Section 1: The Irish Trust Gap

### 1.1 The Cultural Fault Line

Irish business culture operates on what sociologists call a **high-context, relationship-first** communication model. This creates a specific tension for any consultant selling AI services in 2026: the subject matter demands authority signalling (you need to be the smartest person in the room on this topic), but the cultural register demands you never act like it.

The Irish market has a particular disdain for what is colloquially called "the big notions" — visible ambition, aggressive self-promotion, or any copy that sounds like it was written for a Silicon Valley pitch deck. A CMO at a mid-size Irish firm who reads "We transform businesses through cutting-edge AI-powered revenue acceleration" will mentally tag the vendor as American-influenced, probably expensive, possibly full of hot air, and definitely someone to be wary of.

At the same time, that same CMO is under genuine board-level pressure to show they have an AI strategy. They are anxious about being left behind. They are reading about competitors using AI. They went to a BizExpo panel in the RDS and came away more confused than reassured. They need help but they don't want to look like they need help.

This is the trust gap: **a high-anxiety buyer who will reject any signal of over-confidence while desperately needing a confident expert.**

### 1.3 The Anxiety Map

The following anxieties are listed in order of frequency and intensity based on observed patterns in the Irish B2B consultancy market:

**Tier 1 — Universal anxieties (affect all three personas):**
- "Is this person actually good or just good at marketing themselves?"
- "Will I be locked into something I can't get out of?"
- "Is this relevant to a business like mine or just big tech companies?"

**Tier 2 — AI-specific anxieties (heightened in 2025–2026 context):**
- "Is this just a wrapper around ChatGPT?"
- "Will AI replace my team and how do I manage that politically?"
- "The technology is changing so fast — will what they build still be relevant in 12 months?"
- "I don't want to admit I don't understand what GEO/AEO means"

**Tier 3 — Irish-market-specific anxieties:**
- "If this doesn't work, everyone in my network will know"
- "Are they going to disappear to a bigger client once they have me?"
- "Will they understand that Irish consumers are not the same as UK or US consumers?"

---

### 1.4 The 'Cutting Edge vs. Down to Earth' Balance

The positioning sweet spot is not a compromise between these two poles — it is a **sequential revelation**. The site should first establish warmth, specificity, and practicality (earning trust), and then demonstrate depth and expertise (justifying the fee).

A useful frame: think of how a highly respected local GP who also happens to be a published researcher would communicate. They lead with "what's the problem, let's figure this out together" — not "I have seventeen publications." The publications matter. They just come second.

**Copy principle — The 'Pint Test':** Before any piece of copy is published, ask: "Would this sound insane if someone said it to you in a pub?" If yes, rewrite it. "I help businesses show up when their customers are asking AI for recommendations" passes the pint test. "We leverage generative engine optimisation to maximise AI-driven discovery surfaces" does not.

|---|---|---|

| Services nomenclature | Jargon (GEO, AEO) without anchoring | Lead with outcome, bracket with term: "Show up in AI answers (AEO/GEO)" |

## Section 2: AEO/GEO for the Irish Context

### 2.1 The Local-to-Global Intent Stack

Irish consultancy sites face a dual mandate that most IA frameworks are not built for. The site needs to rank and be cited for local searches ("SEO consultant Dublin," "AI strategy Ireland") while simultaneously being credible to international buyers who are searching without geographic qualifiers ("AI content strategy consultant," "GEO optimisation specialist").

These are not just different keywords. They represent different buyer journeys, different trust signals, different page depths, and — critically — different AI citation patterns. A Perplexity user asking "who are the best GEO consultants in Ireland?" and a Gemini user asking "what is generative engine optimisation?" may both land on your site, but they need to find completely different things.

The strategic error most Irish consultants make is trying to serve both audiences from the same pages. The correct approach is a **layered architecture** with a shared conceptual core but divergent entry points.

---

### 2.2 Recommended Information Architecture

```
cillianbc.com/
│
├── / (Homepage)
│   └── Intent: Trust establishment, dual-audience routing
│       Primary signal: "Irish AI consultant" + "GEO/AEO specialist"
│
├── /services/
│   ├── /services/seo/
│   ├── /services/aeo/               ← Answer Engine Optimisation
│   ├── /services/geo/               ← Generative Engine Optimisation
│   ├── /services/content-engineering/
│   └── /services/ai-workflows/
│
├── /work/                           ← Case studies (sector + outcome led)
│   ├── /work/saas-content-strategy/
│   ├── /work/retail-ai-visibility/
│   └── /work/professional-services-aeo/
│
├── /insights/                       ← The trust-building engine
│   ├── /insights/guides/            ← Deep, linkable, citable
│   ├── /insights/commentary/        ← Short-form, timely, Irish context
│   └── /insights/tools/             ← Free tools (AI audit, schema checker)
│
├── /about/
│
├── /for/                            ← Audience-specific entry points
│   ├── /for/irish-smes/
│   ├── /for/marketing-teams/
│   └── /for/international-firms/    ← The global entry point
│

```

The `/for/` section deserves particular attention. This is the architectural solution to the local-to-global problem. Rather than trying to make one page satisfy both the Dublin CMO and the London agency looking for an Irish tech partner, you create explicit routing pages that speak each audience's language while pointing at the same underlying service pages.

### 2.3 URL and Content Clustering Strategy

**Cluster 1: GEO/AEO Authority Cluster**

This cluster positions the site as the Irish reference point for these emerging disciplines. Every URL in this cluster should be answering a question that a CMO might type into Perplexity or Gemini.

```
/insights/guides/what-is-generative-engine-optimisation/
/insights/guides/aeo-vs-seo-whats-the-difference/
/insights/guides/how-to-optimise-for-ai-overviews/
/insights/guides/structured-data-for-ai-search/
/insights/commentary/google-ai-overviews-ireland-2026/
/services/geo/
/services/aeo/
```

The pillar page is `/services/geo/` — but this page does not just sell. It is also the most comprehensive publicly available explanation of GEO in an Irish business context. The cluster pages link to it. The pillar links back to the cluster pages. The whole cluster is internally linked and semantically coherent.

**Cluster 2: Irish AI Search Behaviour Cluster**

This cluster targets the international buyer who is researching the Irish market:

```
/insights/guides/ai-search-behaviour-ireland/
/insights/guides/irish-consumer-ai-adoption-2026/
/for/international-firms/
/insights/commentary/ireland-ai-search-market/
```

These pages serve double duty: they are citable resources for international researchers, and they signal to Irish buyers that this consultant understands the local nuance, not just the global theory.

### 2.5 AI Citation Architecture

For the site to be cited by AI engines (SGE, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude), pages need to be structured around answerable questions, not around the consultant's service menu. The single most important architectural principle for GEO is:

**Every pillar page must contain at least three directly extractable answers to questions a user might ask an AI.**

These should be formatted as discrete, self-contained paragraphs with a bolded question as the heading — not buried in prose. Section 5 covers the technical implementation in detail.

## Section 3: Competitor Audit — Ireland Focus

### 3.1 Audit Scope and Methodology

The Irish digital consultancy market in 2026 sits at an interesting inflection point. The large full-service agencies (Wolfgang Digital, Core, Mediahuis) are adding AI capabilities to existing service stacks. A new tier of independent AI specialists is emerging but lacks the credibility infrastructure of the established players. The positioning gap for an AI-native consultant is real, but it requires careful navigation.

The following audit focuses on three categories: large agency (Wolfgang), mid-tier integrated (Core), and the emerging independent specialist tier.

---

### 3.2 Wolfgang Digital

**Positioning:** "Ireland's most awarded digital marketing agency." Performance-first, data-led, retail and e-commerce focused.

**Strengths relevant to competitive analysis:**
- Extremely strong social proof infrastructure (awards, named clients, published research — particularly their annual E-Commerce KPI report)
- High production value across all content
- Strong LinkedIn presence with genuine industry authority
- Case studies with real revenue numbers, named clients, clear methodology

**AI/Advanced Search offering (as observable):**
- AI capabilities are positioned as tools within existing service lines (PPC automation, content at scale) rather than as a distinct strategic discipline
- No dedicated AEO or GEO service offering
- SEO content largely focused on technical SEO and link acquisition — not structured for AI citation

**Weaknesses from a competitive positioning standpoint:**
- Agency model means clients get a team, not a named expert — relationship dilution
- Primarily Dublin-centric and retail/e-commerce focused
- Content strategy is performative (thought leadership as SEO) rather than genuinely educational
- Size means slower adaptation to emerging disciplines like GEO
- The "awards" positioning is increasingly a commodity signal — every agency in Ireland has DMIAA nominations

**Differentiation opportunity:** Wolfgang is selling a machine. You are selling expertise and judgment. A CMO who wants to understand what AI search means for their specific business — not just have tasks executed — will not be served by Wolfgang. The independent consultant's pitch against Wolfgang is: "I will think with you, not just work for you."

---

### 3.3 Core (Core Optimisation / Core Media Group)

**Positioning:** Full-service media, with data and analytics as a distinct strength. Strong enterprise and government client base.

**Strengths relevant to competitive analysis:**
- Enterprise credibility — major Irish brands, government bodies
- Strong data science capability (relevant to AI positioning)
- Media buying scale creates genuine cross-channel insight
- Dublin 2 presence signals establishment credibility

**AI/Advanced Search offering:**
- Core has invested in data and analytics capabilities that could theoretically extend to AI search
- However, AI strategy as a consultancy offering is not clearly articulated on their public-facing site
- Search offering appears focused on paid search and traditional SEO

**Weaknesses from a competitive positioning standpoint:**
- The integrated agency model means AI/search is always competing internally for attention against media buying revenue
- Not accessible to SMEs or early-stage companies — implicit minimum engagement threshold
- Communication is corporate and formal — low warmth, high friction for first contact
- No visible content around emerging search formats (AI Overviews, Perplexity, conversational search)

**Differentiation opportunity:** Core's enterprise focus leaves a well-funded SME tier (Series A-B companies, professional services, specialist retail) almost completely unaddressed by a credible AI search specialist. The window to own "AI search for Irish growth companies" is open.

---

### 3.4 The Independent Specialist Tier (composite analysis)

The Irish market has a growing number of independent SEO and digital consultants, many of whom are beginning to add "AI" to their service descriptions. Based on observable patterns:

**Common characteristics of this tier:**
- Strong LinkedIn presence but weak website UX
- Service pages that list capabilities without explaining outcomes
- Blog content that is technically competent but poorly structured for AI extraction
- No clear differentiation from each other — the words "SEO, content, strategy, AI" appear interchangeably
- Pricing is implied rather than structured (creates anxiety rather than authority)
- Heavy reliance on referrals, which limits growth ceiling

**What none of them are doing:**
- Explicitly positioning for AEO and GEO as distinct disciplines with their own methodology
- Structuring their own site to demonstrate the capabilities they are selling
- Creating genuinely educational content that earns citations rather than just rankings
- Addressing the Irish-specific context of AI search (cultural nuance, local search behaviour, Irish consumer AI adoption patterns)

This last point is the most significant gap. No independent Irish consultant is currently producing content that addresses how AI search behaves specifically in an Irish context — how Eircode-sensitive searches work in AI Overviews, how Irish business culture affects the types of questions being asked, how Irish consumers differ from UK consumers in AI tool adoption. This is a content territory that can be claimed relatively quickly and is extremely difficult for a large agency to credibly own.

## Section 4: Conversion Sensitivity

### 4.1 The Irish Buyer's Emotional Journey

The Irish B2B buyer does not move in a straight line from awareness to purchase. They loop. They read something, forget about it, see a LinkedIn post three weeks later, remember the site, read two more articles, ask a colleague if they've heard of this person, and then — finally — send a tentative email that they've drafted and deleted twice.

This is not a flaw in the funnel. It is the correct behaviour for a relationship-first buyer making a significant investment. The site's conversion architecture must be designed around this looping, trust-accumulating behaviour rather than trying to shortcut it.

The emotional journey can be mapped in five stages:

---

### 4.2 Emotional Journey Map

**Stage 1: Reluctant Awareness**
*"I probably need to do something about AI but I'm not sure what"*

- Trigger: Board meeting, competitor action, LinkedIn article, conference
- Emotional state: Low-grade anxiety, not yet ready to act
- Behaviour: Passive reading, no intent to contact
- Site touchpoint: Blog post found via search or LinkedIn share
- What they need: To feel understood, not sold to
- Micro-copy principle: Articles should feel like they were written for someone exactly like them. "If you're a marketing manager who's been asked to 'do something with AI' and you're not sure where to start — this is for you."

**Stage 2: Active Research**
*"I should find out who actually knows about this in Ireland"*

- Trigger: A specific problem crystallises (AI Overviews have dropped organic traffic, competitor is using AI for content)
- Emotional state: Curious but guarded, not yet trusting any vendor
- Behaviour: Multiple site visits, reading About page, checking LinkedIn, Googling the consultant's name
- Site touchpoints: Services pages, About page, case studies
- What they need: Specificity and evidence. Not claims, evidence.
- Micro-copy principle: Case studies must include a moment of doubt ("Their traffic had dropped 23% after Google's AI Overview rollout and they weren't sure if it was temporary or structural") followed by a specific resolution.

**Stage 3: Qualified Interest**
*"This person actually knows what they're talking about"*

- Trigger: Reading a piece of content that answers a question they had but didn't know how to ask
- Emotional state: Relief mixed with slight vulnerability (admitting they don't know something)
- Behaviour: Downloading a resource, signing up for a newsletter, saving the page
- Site touchpoints: Lead magnets, newsletter sign-up, contact page
- What they need: A low-stakes next step that doesn't feel like entering a sales process
- Micro-copy principle: Newsletter sign-up copy should not mention "insights" or "updates." It should say something like: "I send occasional emails when I find something genuinely useful. No content calendar, no drip sequences."

**Stage 4: Consideration**
*"I'm thinking about getting in touch but I'm not ready to be sold to"*

- Trigger: A specific project or problem has a budget and a timeline
- Emotional state: Motivated but still cautious — worried about wasting their time and appearing naive
- Behaviour: Re-reading services pages, looking for pricing signals, trying to calibrate the engagement
- Site touchpoints: Services pages, "how this works" section, FAQ
- What they need: Process clarity without pricing pressure
- Micro-copy principle: A "How I Work" section that describes the first conversation as genuinely exploratory. "The first call is about understanding your situation. I'll ask more questions than I answer. If I can help, I'll tell you. If I can't, I'll tell you that too — and point you in the right direction."

**Stage 5: First Contact**
*"I'm going to send an email / book that call"*

- Trigger: The benefit of acting outweighs the anxiety of being sold to
- Emotional state: Slightly exposed — they are asking for help
- Behaviour: Contact form or email
- Site touchpoints: Contact page, calendar booking
- What they need: Immediate reassurance that this was the right decision
- Micro-copy principle: The contact page thank-you message matters more than most people think. "Thanks for getting in touch. I'll read your message properly and come back to you within one working day. No pitch, just a conversation."

### 4.4 Micro-Copy Recommendations

**Homepage headline alternatives (in order of preference):**

1. "I help Irish businesses show up where their customers are searching — including in AI." — *Specific, plain, local, forward-looking*
2. "Most SEO advice was written before AI changed search. I work with what's actually happening now." — *Positions against commodity SEO, implies expertise without claiming it*
3. "AI search is reshaping how businesses get found. I help you stay visible through the shift." — *Stakes-framing, outcome-oriented*

**CTA alternatives — ranked by Irish market appropriateness:**

| CTA Text | Appropriateness | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Book a Discovery Call" | Medium | "Discovery" is slightly jargony but acceptable |
| "Let's Talk" | High | Simple, human, non-committal |
| "Start a Conversation" | High | Implies equality, not a pitch |
| "Get a Free Audit" | Low | "Free" signals commodity; "audit" implies judgement |
| "Schedule a Consultation" | Low | Formal, clinical, US-inflected |
| "Work With Me" | Medium-high | Clear but slightly presumptuous at first touch |
| "See If We're a Good Fit" | High | Reframes the power dynamic — they are also evaluating you |

**Services page opening copy (anti-pattern vs. recommended):**

Anti-pattern:
> "Our cutting-edge GEO and AEO services deliver measurable results for forward-thinking businesses ready to lead in the AI era."

Recommended:
> "Search has changed more in the last 18 months than in the previous decade. If you've noticed your organic traffic behaving differently — appearing in AI summaries, losing clicks even when you rank — that's not a technical glitch. It's the new reality. Here's how I help businesses adapt to it."

**FAQ entries that reduce conversion anxiety:**

- "Do I need to understand AI to work with you?" — No. My job is to translate what's happening in AI search into decisions you can actually act on.
- "How long does this take to show results?" — It depends on the service. AEO work can show citation results in 4–8 weeks. SEO compounding takes longer. I'll give you an honest timeline, not an optimistic one.
- "Do you work with small businesses or only enterprise?" — Both, but differently. I'm not the right fit for everyone. The first conversation usually makes that clear.
- "What happens after the first call?" — Either we agree there's a good fit and I send a straightforward proposal, or we agree there isn't and I'll point you toward someone who's a better match. Either way, you'll leave the call knowing more than when you started.

## Section 5: Technical UX — Structured Data and Semantic Architecture

A consultant who sells GEO/AEO services must have a site that demonstrates those capabilities in practice. This is not just good marketing — it is the most credible proof of competence available. When a prospective client finds the site via a Perplexity answer or a Google AI Overview, the referral source is itself the case study.

The technical architecture below is designed to achieve three things simultaneously: rank in traditional search, be cited by AI engines, and serve as a visible demonstration of the consultant's methodology.

### 5.4 Content Formatting Guide for AI Extractability

**Rule 1: Answer First**

AI engines prefer documents that answer the implied question in the first paragraph. Never "warm up" to the answer. State it, then provide context.

Bad:
> "Search has been evolving for decades, and with the rise of AI tools like ChatGPT and Google's new AI Overview feature, many marketers are wondering what this means for their organic traffic..."

Good:
> "GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) is the practice of structuring content to appear in AI-generated search answers. Here is what Irish businesses need to know."

---

**Rule 2: Use Discrete, Labelled Sections**

Every major point should have its own heading. Headings should be phrased as questions where possible.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "The Landscape is Shifting" | "How has AI changed search in Ireland?" |
| "Our Approach" | "How does this service work?" |
| "Why It Matters" | "Why does GEO matter for Irish businesses?" |
| "Getting Started" | "How do I start with GEO?" |

---

**Rule 3: The Extractable Paragraph**

For every major claim or explanation, write a single paragraph that is self-contained — meaning it makes complete sense if read in isolation, without surrounding context. This is how AI engines extract and cite content.

---

**Rule 4: Lists for Processes and Comparisons**

Structured lists (particularly `<ol>` for sequences and `<ul>` for features) are parsed reliably by AI engines and frequently appear verbatim in AI answers. Use them for:
- Step-by-step processes
- Feature comparisons
- Key takeaways at the end of articles
- "What to look for" type content

---

**Rule 5: Named Entities and Specificity**

AI engines weight content higher when it contains specific, verifiable named entities.

Vague: "Many Irish businesses are seeing changes in their search traffic."
Specific: "Irish B2B companies in professional services sectors saw an average 18% reduction in AI Overview click-through rates between Q3 2025 and Q1 2026, according to data tracked across Cillian BC's client portfolio."

## Summary: Ten Design Principles

1. **People before services** — The site is, above all, a trust document. The consultant's voice, judgment, and presence must be perceptible throughout. Avoid the temptation to make it look like an agency.

2. **Demonstrate what you sell** — If the site does not itself demonstrate excellent GEO, AEO, and content engineering, no amount of copy about those services will be credible. The site is the portfolio.

3. **The pint test** — Every piece of copy must pass the conversational plausibility test. If it would sound absurd said aloud in a normal conversation, rewrite it.

4. **Answer first, explain second** — For AI extractability and for Irish users who are time-poor and slightly sceptical: lead with the answer. Context follows.

5. **Specificity is credibility** — Vague claims reduce trust. Specific details — sectors, outcomes, timelines, named tools — increase it. Replace every instance of "many businesses" with a more precise reference.

6. **Design for the loop, not the funnel** — Irish B2B buyers return to sites multiple times before contacting. Make every return visit more valuable than the last. The insights section must be genuinely worth returning to.

7. **Localise the expertise, globalise the ambition** — The site must signal deep understanding of the Irish market while demonstrating the capability to serve international clients. These are not in tension if the IA is properly structured.

8. **Low-commitment first steps** — Every CTA should feel like an invitation rather than a commitment. The first contact should require less courage than the previous page made it feel like it would.

9. **Schema is not optional** — Structured data on this site is not a technical nicety — it is a core deliverable. Every page type must have appropriate, validated JSON-LD. This is non-negotiable for a GEO practitioner.

10. **The about page is the conversion page** — For solo consultants in relationship-first markets, the About page is frequently the highest-converting page on the site. It must be written with the same strategic intent as any service page — it is not a biography, it is an argument for why this person is the right choice.
