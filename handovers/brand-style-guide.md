# Brand & Visual Style Guide
## cillianbc.com — AI Search Consultancy
### Version 1.0 | March 2026

---

> **A note on what this document is for.** This is not a mood board. It is a decision framework. Every rule here exists because a specific type of Irish B2B buyer needs to feel a specific thing at a specific moment. When in doubt, return to the Central Thesis: *make a CMO in Sandyford feel like they already know and trust the person behind the site before they ever send an email.*

---

## 1. Brand Positioning Statement

This brand serves Irish marketing and business leaders who are smart enough to know that AI is reshaping how their customers find them, and honest enough to admit they don't yet know what to do about it. It stands for genuine expertise delivered without performance — the kind of guidance you'd get from a sharp, trusted colleague rather than a pitch deck. It should feel like the relief of talking to someone who actually knows what they're doing and won't make you feel foolish for asking.

---

## 2. The "Vibe" — Brand Personality & Tone

### Dimension 1: Grounded

**What it means:** This brand has its feet on Irish soil. It is not chasing the Silicon Valley aesthetic, the London agency look, or the continental minimalism of a Figma plugin startup. It exists in the context of Irish business culture — relationship-first, sceptical of hype, quietly impressed by competence.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- *Visually:* Generous whitespace, muted palette, no gradients that look like they were generated at 3am. Typography that reads like a well-set book, not a SaaS landing page.
- *Verbally:* "Here's what I'd actually do" rather than "Our proprietary methodology delivers transformative outcomes." Short sentences. The occasional admission of complexity. No "revolutionising" anything.

**The opposite to avoid:** Aspirational tech-bro positioning. Rocket ships. "10x your visibility." Anything that would make Declan in Kerry roll his eyes and close the tab.

---

### Dimension 2: Precise

**What it means:** Specificity is the brand's primary trust signal. Vague claims ("we help businesses grow") are the signature of consultants who can't prove their work. Every claim is earned, every process is named, every result is specific.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- *Visually:* Consistent spacing, deliberate alignment, no visual clutter. Every element on the page earns its place. Tables and structured content are handled with care — they signal that the consultant thinks in systems.
- *Verbally:* "Organic traffic from AI-cited queries increased 34% in 90 days" rather than "significant improvements to AI visibility." Named services with clear scope. Process steps that can be counted.

**The opposite to avoid:** The generic services page that could belong to anyone. Bullet points that start with "We leverage..." Stock diagrams of funnels and flywheels that mean nothing.

---

### Dimension 3: Warm (Without Performing Warmth)

**What it means:** Irish professional culture treats performed enthusiasm as suspicious. Warmth here is structural — it shows up in how information is organised, in the absence of aggressive sales mechanics, in the sense that the consultant is on your side. It is not exclamation marks and "Let's chat!" CTAs.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- *Visually:* A photograph that looks like a real person in a real place, not a headshot taken by a photographer who also shoots weddings. Generous line height that makes text feel unhurried. A CTA that says "Start a conversation" rather than "Book a discovery call."
- *Verbally:* Acknowledging what the reader might be thinking. "If you're wondering whether this applies to your size of business — yes, it does." The use of "I" throughout (not "we" — there's no "we").

**The opposite to avoid:** Performative friendliness. Chat widgets that pop up within 3 seconds. "Hey there! 👋" in the navigation. Anything that feels like it's trying too hard.

---

### Dimension 4: Quietly Confident

**What it means:** The brand does not need to tell you it's good. It demonstrates competence through specificity, through depth available on demand, through the absence of defensive over-explanation. This maps directly to the Sequential Revelation Principle — warmth first, then depth, never depth first.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- *Visually:* Restraint. No testimonial carousels. No logo walls (unless client logos are genuinely recognisable Irish companies). Technical content — schema examples, methodology diagrams — placed where someone looking for it will find it, not thrown at every visitor.
- *Verbally:* Opinions stated plainly. "Most Irish businesses don't need a full content audit right now — they need three things fixed." No hedging every claim with "potentially" and "could." No false modesty.

**The opposite to avoid:** Either extreme. Bragging (credentialism, vanity metrics, "award-winning") or excessive humility that makes Aoife wonder if this person can justify the spend to her European HQ.

---

### Dimension 5: Irish Without Being Twee

**What it means:** This brand is from here. It understands the Irish business context — the size of the market, the relationship networks, the specific awkwardness around pricing, the fact that your potential client's cousin might work for a competitor. It does not perform Irishness (no shamrocks, no map of Ireland, no "Fáilte" in the navigation).

**What it looks like in practice:**
- *Visually:* The colour palette draws from the Irish landscape without referencing it literally. The typography has a European quality — formal enough for B2B, human enough not to feel American.
- *Verbally:* References to real Irish business contexts. Named Irish organisations when relevant. The language of someone who reads the Irish Times business section, not TechCrunch.

**The opposite to avoid:** Stage-Irish aesthetics. Overt nationalism. Equally — the studied neutrality of a brand that could be from anywhere, which reads as a lack of confidence in the Irish market.

## 3. Colour Palette

### Rationale Before the Hex Codes

The colour system must do three things simultaneously: signal intellectual credibility (for Aoife, who needs to justify this to a European HQ), avoid intimidating Declan (who associates dark, agency-ish palettes with expensive London firms), and feel specific enough that Síobhán doesn't think "I've seen this before." The system is built on a deep Atlantic teal — not blue (too corporate, too generic), not green (too on-the-nose Irish, too startup-y), but the specific blue-green of Irish coastal water on an overcast morning. It reads as grounded, intelligent, and distinctly not generic.

---

### Primary Colour — Atlantic

| Token | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Hex | `#1D4B52` |
| RGB | `29, 75, 82` |
| HSL | `187°, 48%, 22%` |
| WCAG on white | 9.4:1 (AAA) |

**Rationale:** A deep, muted teal that sits at the intersection of trustworthy (the blue family) and distinctive (the green shift). At this depth, it reads as serious without being corporate-navy-generic. It passes AAA contrast on white backgrounds — important for accessibility but also a signal: dark primary text colours suggest a brand that prioritises readability over visual flourish. This will not be confused with Wolfgang Digital's palette, Core's institutional blue, or any SaaS startup currently raising a Series B.

**Primary use:** Navigation, primary headings, primary CTA backgrounds, footer background.

---

### Primary Light Variant — Atlantic Mist

| Token | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Hex | `#EAF2F3` |
| RGB | `234, 242, 243` |
| HSL | `186°, 29%, 94%` |

**Use:** Section backgrounds, card backgrounds, subtle differentiation between content zones. This is the colour of a good Irish morning — not dramatic, just present and calm.

---

### Secondary Colour — Slate

| Token | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Hex | `#3D5560` |
| RGB | `61, 85, 96` |
| HSL | `200°, 22%, 31%` |
| WCAG on white | 7.1:1 (AAA) |

**Rationale:** A desaturated blue-grey that provides visual range within the primary family. Used for secondary headings, secondary UI elements, and hover states. Creates depth without introducing a new hue. Declan reads this as "professional without showing off." Síobhán reads it as "consistent system — this person sweats the details."

---

### Accent Colour — Amber

| Token | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Hex | `#C97D2E` |
| RGB | `201, 125, 46` |
| HSL | `34°, 63%, 48%` |
| WCAG on white | 3.2:1 (AA Large only) |

**Rationale:** Warm amber — not orange (too startup), not gold (too finance), but the specific tone of Irish oak or a well-worn leather notebook. This is the brand's warmth register made visual. Used extremely sparingly — maximum 5% of any screen's visual weight — because it functions as emphasis, and emphasis only works when it's rare. When Síobhán's eye lands on an amber element, it means something: this is important, or this is the human moment on the page.

**Why it should be used sparingly:** This colour has the lowest contrast ratio in the system (3.2:1 — AA Large only, meaning it passes for text at 18pt+ or bold 14pt+ but NOT for body text). This is a feature, not a bug. It enforces restraint. Never use amber for body text. Never use it on the Atlantic primary background. Use it for: inline highlights in blockquotes, selected state indicators, small decorative rules above section headings, the occasional icon accent.

---

### Neutral / Background System

| Name | Hex | HSL | Use |
|------|-----|-----|-----|
| Off-White | `#F7F6F2` | `40°, 20%, 97%` | Page background — not pure white (too clinical, too unfinished) |
| Warm Stone | `#EEECE6` | `42°, 17%, 92%` | Alternate section backgrounds, card fills |
| Mid Stone | `#D6D2C8` | `42°, 13%, 81%` | Dividers, borders, subtle separators |
| Deep Stone | `#8C887F` | `40°, 6%, 54%` | Placeholder text, secondary captions, metadata |

**Why off-white instead of pure white:** Pure white (`#FFFFFF`) creates excessive contrast against the Atlantic primary and reads as the default — the visual equivalent of not making a decision. The warm off-white (`#F7F6F2`) has a slight warmth that makes long-form text more comfortable to read and subtly signals that someone has thought carefully about the reading experience. Síobhán reads longform content. She will notice.

---

### Text Colours

|------|-----|-----|-------------------|

| Secondary Text | `#4A4742` | Supporting copy, labels, metadata | 8.1:1 (AAA) |
| Muted Text | `#8C887F` | Captions, timestamps, disabled states | 3.5:1 (AA Large) |

---

### Semantic Colours

| State | Hex | Rationale |
|-------|-----|-----------|
| Success | `#2A6B4A` | Deep forest green — success feels solid, not celebratory |
| Warning | `#A0631A` | Deep amber-brown — warning without alarm, consistent with the warm palette |
| Error | `#8B2A2A` | Deep burgundy-red — serious, not hysterical |
| Info | `#1D4B52` | Atlantic primary — informational states use the brand colour |

---

### Forbidden Colour Combinations

- Amber on Atlantic primary (fails WCAG AA at body size)
- Muted text (`#8C887F`) on Warm Stone (contrast drops below 3:1)
- Any pure colour (100% saturated) anywhere in the system
- Dark text on Mid Stone for anything smaller than 16px

## 4. Typography System

### Rationale Before the Typefaces

Typography for this brand must resolve an inherent tension: Cillian is a technical specialist (suggesting precision, possibly monospace-influenced choices) who is also a trusted human advisor (suggesting warmth, readability, and approachability). The system uses three typefaces, each with a clear domain, and they must never compete with each other. The guiding principle is *a well-designed journal from a serious publisher* — not a tech blog, not a corporate intranet.

---

### Primary Typeface — Headings & Brand Moments

**[Fraunces](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Fraunces)**
*Weights in use: 300 (Light), 600 (SemiBold), 700 (Bold)*
*Optical Size range: 9 to 144 — use optical-size CSS feature*
*Style: Roman only (no italic in headings)*

**Rationale:** Fraunces is a "wonky" optical serif with strong historical roots and a distinctive personality — it reads as both authoritative and individual, which is the exact tension this brand must resolve. It is not Garamond (too academic), not Georgia (too web-default), not Playfair Display (too editorial-magazine). Fraunces has a quiet idiosyncrasy that prevents it from feeling corporate without tipping into eccentric. The optical sizing feature means it remains readable at small sizes and expressive at large sizes without requiring different typefaces. For Irish B2B buyers: Síobhán will register it as design-forward without being showy. Declan won't consciously notice it, but he'll feel that the site takes itself seriously. Aoife will recognise it as a considered choice that signals European design awareness.

**Primary use:** H1–H3, hero statements, section titles, pull quotes.

---

### Secondary / Body Typeface — Prose & UI

**[Inter](https://rsms.me/inter/)**
*Weights in use: 400 (Regular), 500 (Medium), 600 (SemiBold)*
*Feature settings: `cv02, cv03, cv04, cv11` (disambiguate similar characters), `ss01` (alternate digits)*

**Rationale:** Inter is the honest choice, and honesty is a core brand value. It is not chosen because it is fashionable — it is chosen because it is optimised for screen reading across all viewport sizes and device densities, it has exceptional OpenType feature support, and its neutral quality allows Fraunces to be the personality and Inter to be the workhorse. The specific feature settings enable `a` and `g` forms that are more legible and less generic. Using Inter with intentional feature settings signals that the designer (and by extension the consultant) cares about details that most people don't notice.

**Primary use:** Body copy, navigation, UI labels, form fields, H4–H6, metadata, all prose at 16px and below.

---

### Monospace Typeface — Technical Content

**[JetBrains Mono](https://www.jetbrains.com/lp/mono/)**
*Weights in use: 400 (Regular), 500 (Medium)*

**Rationale:** This is a consultancy that works with structured data, schema markup, and AI outputs. Code blocks, JSON-LD examples, and technical specifications will appear on the site. JetBrains Mono has a high x-height, deliberate letterform distinction (zero vs. O, one vs. l vs. I), and a reading rhythm that works well in longer technical passages. It is not Courier (dated), not Roboto Mono (too clinical), not Fira Code (ligatures can confuse non-developers). For Aoife especially — the presence of well-set technical content signals that the consultant can hold their own in a technical conversation without needing to be managed.

**Primary use:** Code blocks, inline code references, technical specifications, schema examples.

---

### Type Scale

All values in `rem` (base: 16px = 1rem). The scale follows a modified Major Third ratio (1.25) for body sizes and shifts to a more expressive ratio at display sizes.

| Token | Size | Typeface | Weight | Line Height | Letter Spacing | Use |
|-------|------|----------|--------|-------------|----------------|-----|
| `--text-display` | 4rem (64px) | Fraunces | 300 | 1.1 | -0.02em | Hero statements, one per page |
| `--text-h1` | 2.75rem (44px) | Fraunces | 600 | 1.2 | -0.015em | Page titles |
| `--text-h2` | 2rem (32px) | Fraunces | 600 | 1.25 | -0.01em | Section headings |
| `--text-h3` | 1.5rem (24px) | Fraunces | 600 | 1.3 | -0.005em | Sub-section headings |
| `--text-h4` | 1.125rem (18px) | Inter | 600 | 1.4 | 0em | Card titles, sidebar headings |
| `--text-h5` | 1rem (16px) | Inter | 600 | 1.5 | 0.01em | Labels, small headings |
| `--text-h6` | 0.875rem (14px) | Inter | 600 | 1.5 | 0.03em | Eyebrows, category tags |
| `--text-body-lg` | 1.125rem (18px) | Inter | 400 | 1.7 | 0em | Intro paragraphs, hero body |
| `--text-body` | 1rem (16px) | Inter | 400 | 1.65 | 0em | Standard body copy |
| `--text-body-sm` | 0.875rem (14px) | Inter | 400 | 1.6 | 0.01em | Supporting copy, sidebar text |
| `--text-caption` | 0.75rem (12px) | Inter | 500 | 1.5 | 0.02em | Image captions, timestamps |
| `--text-code` | 0.9375rem (15px) | JetBrains Mono | 400 | 1.6 | 0em | Inline and block code |
| `--text-label` | 0.75rem (12px) | Inter | 600 | 1.4 | 0.08em | ALL-CAPS UI labels only |

**Note on ALL-CAPS labels:** Permissible only at `--text-label` size and only with 0.08em letter spacing. Never apply uppercase transforms to Fraunces. Never use ALL-CAPS for body copy or headings.

---

### Typography Usage Rules

**Permitted combinations:**
- Fraunces heading + Inter body (primary pairing — use everywhere)
- Inter heading (H4–H6) + Inter body (secondary pairing — use in UI components)
- JetBrains Mono in code blocks within Inter or Fraunces prose contexts

**Forbidden combinations:**
- Fraunces for body copy at any size below 20px (becomes precious and difficult)
- JetBrains Mono for anything other than technical/code content
- Mixing Fraunces and Inter in the same heading level
- Bold Fraunces at display size — use Light (300) for display, SemiBold/Bold for H1–H3 only
- Italic type for the primary typeface in headings (Fraunces italic exists but belongs in pull quotes and emphasis within prose only)

## 5. Spacing & Layout Principles

### Grid System

**12-column grid** with the following configuration:

| Breakpoint | Columns | Gutter | Margin | Max Content Width |
|------------|---------|--------|--------|-------------------|
| Mobile (< 640px) | 4 | 16px | 20px | 100% |
| Tablet (640–1024px) | 8 | 24px | 32px | 100% |
| Desktop (1024–1440px) | 12 | 32px | 48px | 1200px |
| Wide (> 1440px) | 12 | 32px | Auto | 1200px |

**Prose width constraint:** All longform text content — body copy, articles, service descriptions — is capped at **68 characters per line** (approximately `42rem` or `672px`). This is non-negotiable. Beyond 75 characters, reading becomes fatiguing. The optimal reading measure also signals that the brand cares about the reading experience — which is a direct expression of "I'm on your side."

**Content zone widths:**
- Narrow (prose): `42rem` max
- Standard (components): `60rem` max
- Full (hero, full-bleed sections): `75rem` max

---

### Whitespace Philosophy

The brand's whitespace is generous, deliberate, and asymmetrical. It is not centered-and-equal-spaced in the manner of a template. The guiding principle is: **whitespace is not emptiness — it is breathing room for trust to form.**

For Declan: generous whitespace signals that this consultant is not trying to overwhelm him. Every paragraph has room. He does not feel sold at.

For Síobhán: generous whitespace signals confidence. Brands that are insecure about their value proposition fill every pixel. This brand doesn't need to.

For Aoife: well-structured whitespace signals systematic thinking. The spacing is clearly grid-derived, not accidental.

**Rule:** When in doubt, add more whitespace, not less. The only exception is in technical documentation where density serves the reader's purpose.

---

### Spacing Tokens

Based on a 4px base unit, following a modular scale:

| Token | Value | Use |
|-------|-------|-----|
| `--space-xs` | `4px` (0.25rem) | Icon-to-label gaps, tight inline elements |
| `--space-sm` | `8px` (0.5rem) | Internal component padding (compact), list item gaps |
| `--space-md` | `16px` (1rem) | Standard component padding, paragraph gaps |
| `--space-lg` | `24px` (1.5rem) | Card padding, form field spacing |
| `--space-xl` | `40px` (2.5rem) | Between related content sections |
| `--space-2xl` | `64px` (4rem) | Between major page sections |
| `--space-3xl` | `96px` (6rem) | Hero sections, dramatic breathing room |
| `--space-4xl` | `128px` (8rem) | Maximum section separation on desktop |

**Section padding rule:** All major page sections use `--space-2xl` minimum top and bottom padding on desktop, `--space-xl` on tablet, `--space-lg` on mobile.

## 6. Visual Language & Imagery

### Photography Style

Photography — if used — must pass a single test: **does this look like it was taken in Ireland, or does it look like it was downloaded from Unsplash?**

**What to use:**
- A single portrait photograph of Cillian, taken with natural light, in a real working context (not a studio). The background should be recognisably Irish — not postcard-Irish, but a real office, a real café, a real desk with actual books on it. The subject should be looking slightly off-camera — the "thinking" look rather than the "sold you on me" look.
- Irish landscape as texture only (never as illustration of the business). A desaturated photograph of the Irish coastline used as a full-bleed section background, with the Atlantic colour overlaid at 70% opacity, works as a grounding device. This should happen at most once per page.
- No team photos (there is no team). No office photos (unless it's genuinely a distinctive workspace). No stock photos of "collaboration" or "meetings."

**Client logos, if used:** In grayscale only, at 60% opacity, set against the Off-White background. Never on a dark background (it looks like you're trying too hard to be impressive). No more than 6 logos. Only Irish-recognisable clients.

---

### Illustration & Iconography

**Iconography system:**
- Use a single, consistent icon library: **Lucide** (or equivalent clean, 1.5px stroke weight, 24px grid). Never mix icon libraries.
- Icons are decorative support, never load-bearing. If removing an icon breaks comprehension, the copy is wrong.
- Icons in navigation: permissible only for utility (search, menu, external link). Never illustrative icons next to service names.
- All icons in `--color-slate` or `--color-atlantic` on light backgrounds. Never in accent amber except for specific selected/highlight states.

**Illustration:**
- No illustration on this site. This is a deliberate absence. Illustration is a personality signal — the wrong illustration style will undermine the precise, grounded brand more than no illustration at all. The brand's visual personality is expressed through typography, spacing, and photography, not illustration.
- The one exception: simple, hand-drawn-style diagrams for methodology explanations. These must look like something a knowledgeable person drew in a meeting to explain a concept, not a Figma plugin output. Black ink on white, scanned or digitised to preserve texture.

---

### What to Avoid — Visual Clichés of the Tech Consultancy World

This list is specific and non-negotiable:

1. **The rocket ship.** Never. Not as an icon, not as a metaphor, not in copy.
2. **Gradient backgrounds** in purple-to-pink or blue-to-teal. This is the uniform of every SaaS startup. It reads as "we spent three hours on the website."
3. **The floating laptop mockup.** Or any device mockup where a screenshot is shown floating at an angle against a gradient.
4. **The "our process" diagram** with four circles connected by arrows. If the process needs to be shown, show it as a numbered list with prose explanation.
5. **Abstract 3D shapes** (orbs, blobs, geometric forms floating in the hero). These are everywhere in AI/tech branding and communicate nothing.
6. **The megaphone or lightbulb icon** next to any service name.
7. **Stock photography of hands shaking**, people at whiteboards, or "diverse team laughing at laptop."

---

### Data Visualisation Principles

When Cillian presents data — ranking trends, visibility metrics, AI citation rates — the visualisation must feel like it was made by someone who understands data, not by someone who found a nice chart plugin.

**Rules:**
- **Minimal chartjunk.** No 3D charts. No pie charts (use a bar chart or a simple proportional statement in prose). No unnecessary gridlines.
- **Colour usage in charts:** Atlantic primary for the primary data series. Slate for secondary series. Amber for the one data point that matters most in the series. Never more than three colours in a single chart.
- **Annotations over legends.** Label data series directly where possible; a legend requires the reader to do extra work. This is not a small thing — it signals whether the communicator is thinking about the reader's experience.
- **Source attribution always visible** — even if it's Cillian's own data. "Source: client search console data, anonymised" builds more trust than unlabelled charts.
- **Axis labels in `--text-caption` size**, in `--color-secondary-text`. The data should lead; the structure should recede.

## 7. UI Component Principles

### Button Hierarchy

**Primary Button**
- Background: `--color-atlantic` (`#1D4B52`)
- Text: `--color-inverted-text` (`#F7F6F2`)
- Padding: `--space-md` vertical, `--space-xl` horizontal (16px / 40px)
- Border radius: `4px` — deliberately minimal. This is not a pill button. A slight radius softens without becoming friendly-tech.
- Font: Inter 500, `--text-body` (16px), no uppercase transformation
- Hover state: `--color-slate` background, 200ms ease transition
- Active state: 98% scale transform, 150ms
- Copy rule: Verb-first, specific. "Start a conversation" not "Submit." "Get the audit" not "Learn more."
- **Maximum one primary CTA per viewport.** This rule enforces the restraint principle.

**Secondary Button**
- Background: transparent
- Border: 1.5px solid `--color-atlantic`
- Text: `--color-atlantic`
- All other properties: same as primary
- Hover: `--color-atlantic-mist` background
- Use: Secondary actions on the same screen as a primary button. Service detail links. "Read the case study" alongside "Start a conversation."

**Ghost Button / Text Link with Arrow**
- No border, no background
- Text: `--color-atlantic`, Inter 500
- Right-pointing arrow (→) that translates 4px right on hover, 200ms ease
- Use: Tertiary actions, navigation between related content, "Read more" links in prose contexts

**Forbidden button patterns:**
- Buttons with emoji or icon decorations (exception: the arrow on ghost buttons)
- "CTA red" or high-contrast accent buttons (amber is insufficient contrast for CTA use)
- Rounded pill buttons (border-radius > 6px on any button)
- ALL-CAPS button text at body size

---

### Card / Surface Treatment

Cards are used sparingly — only when content genuinely benefits from visual containment (services, case study summaries, process steps).

**Standard card:**
- Background: `--color-off-white` or `--color-warm-stone`
- Border: 1px solid `--color-mid-stone`
- Border radius: `6px`
- Padding: `--space-lg` (24px) on all sides, `--space-xl` (40px) on desktop for featured cards
- No box shadow on the default state (see Shadow section)
- Hover: `1px solid --color-slate` border, subtle lift shadow (see below)

**Elevated / Featured card:**
- Background: `--color-atlantic-mist`
- Left border: `3px solid --color-atlantic` (used for testimonials, key methodology steps)
- No outer shadow on elevated cards — the border treatment provides enough visual hierarchy

---

### Border Radius Philosophy

This brand uses minimal, consistent border radius. The philosophy: **radius should soften, not sweeten.**

| Context | Radius |
|---------|--------|
| Buttons | `4px` |
| Cards | `6px` |
| Images | `4px` |
| Form fields | `4px` |
| Code blocks | `6px` |
| Tags/chips | `3px` |
| Full pill | Never |

**No border-radius above 8px on any structural element.** Pill-shaped elements (border-radius: 9999px) are reserved for status badges only, and even then, used sparingly.

---

### Shadow / Elevation Approach

Shadows are used to indicate interaction state, not visual interest. The default page surface has no shadows.

| Token | Value | Use |
|-------|-------|-----|
| `--shadow-sm` | `0 1px 3px rgba(29, 75, 82, 0.08)` | Card hover state |
| `--shadow-md` | `0 4px 12px rgba(29, 75, 82, 0.10)` | Floating elements (dropdowns, tooltips) |
| `--shadow-lg` | `0 8px 24px rgba(29, 75, 82, 0.12)` | Modal/drawer backgrounds |
| `--shadow-focus` | `0 0 0 3px rgba(29, 75, 82, 0.25)` | Focus ring for keyboard navigation |

**Shadow colour is always derived from the Atlantic primary, never from pure black.** This keeps the shadow system cohesive with the palette and prevents the visual "muddiness" of generic black box-shadows.

---

### Form Field Design Principles

Forms on this site are contact forms and enquiry forms — high-stakes interactions where the visitor has decided to trust Cillian enough to make contact. The form must not introduce friction or anxiety at this moment.

- **Labels above fields, always.** Never placeholder-as-label. When a user starts typing, they shouldn't have to remember what the field is for.
- **Field styling:** 1px `--color-mid-stone` border, `4px` radius, `--space-md` padding (16px), `--text-body` size
- **Focus state:** `--shadow-focus` ring, border colour shifts to `--color-atlantic`
- **Error state:** `--color-error` border, error message below the field in `--text-body-sm`, prefixed with an icon. Never red text above the field ("Error: invalid email") — that's accusatory.
- **Field width:** Never full page width. Maximum `480px` for a single-column form field. Full width on mobile only.
- **Submit button:** Primary button style, right-aligned (not full-width). The visual position signals a journey — you've moved from left (input) to right (action).
- **No required asterisks** if every field is required. If only some fields are required, mark the optional ones "(optional)" in secondary text.

## 8. Brand in Motion — Micro-interaction Principles

### Animation Philosophy

Motion on this site communicates one thing: **thoughtfulness.** It does not entertain. It does not demonstrate technical capability. It does not draw attention to itself. Motion is how the brand says "I noticed that you did something, and I want to respond in a considered way."

For Irish B2B buyers, motion that is too fast feels cheap; motion that is too slow feels like it doesn't respect their time. Motion that calls attention to itself feels like the brand is performing — which is exactly what this brand must never do.

**The governing principle: motion should be felt but not noticed.**

---

### Transition Timing Guidelines

| Context | Duration | Easing | Rationale |
|---------|----------|--------|-----------|
| Hover states (colour, border) | 150ms | `ease` | Responsive enough to feel immediate |
| Page element reveals | 400ms | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` | Ease-out feels natural, not mechanical |
| Navigation open/close | 250ms | `ease-in-out` | Balanced — the user is waiting for this |
| Modal/drawer | 300ms | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` | Slightly slower, significant UI change |
| Ghost button arrow | 200ms | `ease` | The micro-moment of directional movement |
| Focus ring appearance | 100ms | `ease-out` | Must feel immediate for keyboard users |
| Scroll-triggered reveals | 600ms | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` | Content entering the viewport is an event |

**Scroll-triggered animation rule:** Elements entering the viewport translate from `translateY(16px)` to `translateY(0)`, with `opacity: 0` to `opacity: 1`. Maximum displacement is 16px — this brand does not do dramatic scroll animations. Elements should appear to settle, not fly.

---

### What to Never Animate

1. **Text content during loading.** Text that letter-animates, word-by-word appears, or types itself out is a user experience failure masquerading as personality.
2. **The hero heading.** It must be visible and readable immediately. No delay. No entrance animation on the most important line of copy on the page.
3. **Navigation items.** Navigation should be instantaneous. Any latency in navigation (even 100ms) erodes trust in the site's responsiveness.
4. **Looping/ambient animations.** No rotating elements, no pulsing dots, no animated backgrounds. The page should be still when the user is still.
5. **Layout shifts during animation.** Motion must never cause reflow. Stick to `transform` and `opacity` only — never animate `height`, `width`, `padding`, or `margin`.
6. **Anything not driven by user intent.** Auto-playing carousels, auto-advancing testimonials, timed popups. If the user didn't initiate it, it doesn't move.

**Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.** All animations must be disabled or reduced when the user has indicated a preference for reduced motion. This is both an accessibility requirement and a brand requirement — a brand that overrides the user's preference is telling them their settings don't matter.

## 9. Do / Don't Examples

### 1. The Hero Headline

**DO:**
> "I help Irish businesses stay visible as search shifts from links to AI answers."

This is specific (Irish businesses), honest about the problem (search is shifting), and unpretentious in its claim.

**DON'T:**
> "Future-Proof Your Digital Presence with AI-First Search Solutions."

This fails the Pint Test immediately. It uses three pieces of jargon ("future-proof," "digital presence," "AI-First Search Solutions"), makes no specific claim, could belong to any company anywhere, and activates Síobhán's jargon detector within the first read. Declan reads "digital presence" and closes the tab.

---

### 2. Service Naming

**DO:**
> **AI Visibility Audit** — A structured review of how your business currently appears (or doesn't) in AI-generated answers, and what's preventing you from being cited.

**DON'T:**
> **GEO360™ Accelerator Package** — Comprehensive generative engine optimisation strategy deployment across your full digital ecosystem.

The second example commits four errors: a trademarked-looking acronym (nobody asked for this), a word ("Accelerator") that belongs in startup pitch decks, the word "Package" which signals that the consultant buys things in bulk, and copy so abstract it describes nothing. Declan doesn't know what a "digital ecosystem" is and doesn't feel like asking.

---

### 3. The Social Proof Section

**DO:**
> "Before working with Cillian, we were invisible in AI search results. Six months later, our brand is cited by ChatGPT and Perplexity when people ask about [specific topic in our industry]. That's real — I can see it in our pipeline."
> — *Head of Marketing, Irish SaaS company (name shared on request)*

Specific outcome. Specific tools named. Honest acknowledgment of privacy. The parenthetical "(name shared on request)" is a trust-builder — it signals that real references exist and are available.

**DON'T:**
> "★★★★★ Cillian is an absolute game-changer. His holistic approach to our AI search strategy transformed our entire digital footprint. We can't recommend him highly enough!"
> — *Happy Client*

Five stars and "Happy Client" are so hollow they actively undermine trust. "Game-changer," "holistic," "digital footprint" are the words of someone who was asked for a testimonial and couldn't think of anything specific. Aoife needs to justify the spend to her HQ — she cannot do so with this.

---

### 4. The Services Page Section for Declan

**DO:**
> **Is this for a business like mine?**
> Most of my clients are Irish SMEs with a real marketing function — a team of one to five people who are managing more than they can reasonably handle. If you have an existing website that gets some traffic but you're not sure how AI tools are affecting your visibility, this is probably a relevant conversation.

This anticipates Declan's anxiety directly, normalises the situation (you're not alone), uses plain language ("a real marketing function," "more than they can reasonably handle"), and avoids any commitment language ("probably a relevant conversation," not "you need this immediately").

**DON'T:**
> **Who is this service for?**
> Our comprehensive GEO and AEO optimisation services are designed for forward-thinking SMEs across all sectors who are ready to embrace the AI-first future of search marketing.

"Forward-thinking" implies that not buying is backwards. "All sectors" means this person knows nothing about Declan's specific business. "Ready to embrace" is a commitment-pressure phrase. This copy exists to make the consultant feel good, not to serve the reader.

---

### 5. The CTA at the Bottom of a Service Page

**DO:**
> **Curious whether this applies to your situation?**
> I do a free, no-obligation 30-minute call with any Irish business that's seriously considering this. No deck, no pitch — just a conversation to see if there's something worth exploring. [Start a conversation →]

Structured around the reader's hesitation. Removes multiple anxiety points explicitly ("no-obligation," "no deck, no pitch"). The word "seriously" gently pre-qualifies without feeling exclusionary. The arrow on the CTA is the only movement; the rest is still.

**DON'T:**
> **Ready to transform your search visibility?**
> Book your FREE Discovery Call now and let's unlock your business's AI search potential! [CLAIM YOUR SPOT →]

"Transform" and "unlock potential" are performative. "FREE" in caps implies scarcity or high-pressure tactics. "CLAIM YOUR SPOT" implies there's a queue and you might miss out — Síobhán's first thought is "miss out on what?" The exclamation mark fails the Pint Test. This reads like a webinar ad.

---

### 6. Data Presentation

**DO:**
Present a simple line chart showing organic traffic trend for an anonymised client. The chart has a direct label at the end of the line: "34% increase at month 6." One annotation marks when structured data was implemented. Source: "Client Google Search Console data, anonymised with client permission."

**DON'T:**
Present the same data as a 3D pie chart with purple, teal, yellow and orange slices showing "Traffic Sources." Add a legend in a separate box. Add a gradient background to the chart area. Put "Data powered by DataDrivenInsights™" in the footer.

---

### 7. The "About" Page Opener

**DO:**
> I'm Cillian. I've spent the last [X] years helping Irish businesses get found online — first through traditional SEO, then through content strategy, and now through whatever we've decided to call it when an AI answers a question and your business either shows up or doesn't.

First person. Named. A timeline that implies experience without listing years. A moment of dry humour ("whatever we've decided to call it") that passes the Pint Test. The phrase "your business either shows up or doesn't" is the clearest possible statement of the problem.

**DON'T:**
> Cillian is a leading Irish AI Search Consultant and Digital Visibility Expert with over a decade of experience helping businesses achieve sustainable growth through cutting-edge search methodologies.

"Leading" is unverifiable and unconvincing. Third person on a one-person business's About page is strange. Every noun is abstract. "Sustainable growth through cutting-edge search methodologies" contains four words Declan would never use in a sentence and two that don't mean anything specific.

## A Final Word on Using This Guide

Every rule in this document exists in service of one goal: making Síobhán, Declan, and Aoife feel, before they have sent a single email, that they have found exactly the right person for a problem they didn't quite know how to articulate. The brand achieves this not by announcing its qualities but by embodying them — in the specificity of the copy, the restraint of the palette, the generosity of the whitespace, the absence of everything that doesn't need to be there.

The most important sentence in this entire guide is the one from the Central Thesis: *Irish B2B buyers buy people before they buy services.* Every visual and verbal decision must earn that trust. When a decision doesn't serve that goal, the guide says no — and when it does, the guide gets out of the way.

Use these tokens. Respect the hierarchy. Do the work.
