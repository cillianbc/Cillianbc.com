# Handover Document: The Soul of the Project
## User Personas, Value Proposition & Brand DNA
**Project:** cillianbc.com — AI Search Consultancy, Ireland
**Date:** March 2026
**Purpose:** This document captures the emotional and strategic core of the project. It should inform every design, copy, and content decision made on the site.

---

## The Central Thesis

> **Irish B2B buyers buy people before they buy services.**

The website's primary job is not to explain what GEO is. It is to make a CMO in Sandyford feel like they already know and trust the person behind the site before they ever send an email.

This is a solo consultancy in a relationship-first market, selling technically complex services to buyers who are under pressure, sceptical of hype, and deeply suspicious of anyone with "big notions." The entire UX, copy, and content strategy flows from this one truth.

---

## The Trust Gap (The Core Problem to Solve)

Irish business culture operates on a **high-context, relationship-first** communication model. For a consultant selling AI services in 2026, this creates a specific tension:

- The subject matter demands authority signalling (you need to be the smartest person in the room on this topic)
- The cultural register demands you never act like it

The Irish market has a particular disdain for visible ambition, aggressive self-promotion, or any copy that sounds like it was written for a Silicon Valley pitch deck.

At the same time, every CMO and business owner is under genuine board-level pressure to show they have an AI strategy. They are anxious about being left behind. They need help — but they don't want to look like they need help.

**This is the trust gap: a high-anxiety buyer who will reject any signal of over-confidence while desperately needing a confident expert.**

The site must resolve this tension through restraint, specificity, and warmth — not through credentialism or superlatives.

## The Three Core Personas

### Persona 1: Síobhán
**Head of Marketing, Series B SaaS Company, Dublin**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 38 |
| **Company size** | 60–120 employees |
| **Situation** | Has been told by her CEO to "do something with AI" for content and SEO |
| **Primary anxiety** | Being sold something complex she can't explain to her board |
| **Secondary anxiety** | Paying a lot for something that turns out to be ChatGPT with a nice deck |
| **How she finds consultants** | LinkedIn first, website second, asks in her network third |
| **What she tells herself** | "I just want a conversation first, I'm not committing to anything" |

**Core emotional driver: Control.**
She needs to feel she understands what she's buying and can defend it internally. The site must give her language she can use in her next leadership meeting.

**What the site must do for Síobhán:**
Every service page must answer the implicit question: *"What would I tell my CEO this person does?"* The answer must fit in one sentence.

**Trust signals she responds to:** Specific results, named Irish clients (even if anonymised by sector), plain language, evidence that the consultant has worked with companies at her stage.

**Trust signals that trigger rejection:** Jargon-heavy positioning, US-style testimonial carousels, aggressive CTAs, anything that implies she should already know what GEO means.

### Persona 2: Declan
**Managing Director, 15-person Kerry-based Manufacturing Company**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 52 |
| **Company size** | 15–40 employees |
| **Situation** | Was told by his accountant that AI could reduce his content costs |
| **Primary anxiety** | Being taken advantage of by someone he can't verify locally |
| **Secondary anxiety** | Technology disrupting his team before he understands it |
| **How he finds consultants** | Google search, local business network, Enterprise Ireland recommendations |

**Core emotional driver: Safety.**
He is not an early adopter. He wants to know this has worked for someone like him before he commits anything.

**What the site must do for Declan:**
Case studies must include enough specificity to feel real — sector, challenge, rough scale of result — without naming clients. *"A Munster-based manufacturer"* is more trustworthy to Declan than *"a leading Irish enterprise."*

**Trust signals he responds to:** Plain English throughout, no contracts mentioned upfront, a consultant who asks questions before pitching, evidence of working with businesses at his scale and in his sector.

**Trust signals that trigger rejection:** London or US pricing language, complex service taxonomies, "AI strategy" buzzwords without grounding, anything that assumes he already has a digital team.

| | |
|---|---|

## The Three-Tier Anxiety Map

Understanding what keeps these personas up at night is as important as understanding what attracts them. Every piece of site content should implicitly address one or more of these anxieties.

### Tier 1 — Universal Anxieties (all three personas)
- *"Is this person actually good or just good at marketing themselves?"*
- *"Will I be locked into something I can't get out of?"*
- *"Is this relevant to a business like mine or just big tech companies?"*

### Tier 2 — AI-Specific Anxieties (heightened in 2025–2026)
- *"Is this just a wrapper around ChatGPT?"*
- *"Will AI replace my team and how do I manage that politically?"*
- *"The technology is changing so fast — will what they build still be relevant in 12 months?"*
- *"I don't want to admit I don't understand what GEO/AEO means"*

### Tier 3 — Irish-Market-Specific Anxieties
- *"If this doesn't work, everyone in my network will know"*
- *"Are they going to disappear to a bigger client once they have me?"*
- *"Will they understand that Irish consumers are not the same as UK or US consumers?"*

## The Value Proposition

### Core Statement

> **"I help Irish businesses stay visible as search moves from links to AI answers — without the jargon, without the lock-in, and without pretending the technology is simpler than it is."**

This is not a tagline. It is the internal compass for every piece of copy on the site. A tagline can evolve; this statement should not.

### What This Means in Practice

| Claim | What it actually means | How it shows up on site |
|---|---|---|
| **Irish-specific** | Deep understanding of Irish business culture, search behaviour, and the local market — not just global theory applied here | Local case studies, Irish-specific content cluster, cultural register in all copy |
| **Stay visible** | Not just traditional SEO — presence in AI Overviews, Perplexity, Gemini citations, and the next shift after that | GEO and AEO positioned as the core offering, not an add-on |
| **Without the jargon** | Explaining complex concepts in plain English, without dumbing them down | Every service page written so Declan from Kerry can understand it; every FAQ written so Síobhán can repeat it to her CEO |
| **Without the lock-in** | Independent advice, no vendor relationships, transparent process | "How I work" section, explicit no-retainer-pressure language |
| **Without pretending** | Intellectual honesty — stating clearly when AI isn't the right tool, when timelines are uncertain, when results depend on factors outside the consultant's control | Honest timelines in FAQs, realistic case study framing |

## The Positioning Sweet Spot

The consultant occupies a position no other player in the Irish market currently holds:

```
                    AGENCY SCALE
                         │
        Wolfgang         │         Core
        (Machine)        │    (Institution)
                         │
─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────
  GENERALIST             │              SPECIALIST
                         │
    Independent          │         ★ HERE ★
    Consultants          │    (Person-led +
    (Warm but generic)   │    technically deep)
                         │
                    SOLO / PERSONAL
```

The sweet spot: **the warmth and accessibility of an independent consultant, combined with verifiable technical depth in the specific disciplines AI is disrupting.**

This combination is rare. The site must make it legible at a glance.

## The Brand Voice: The 'Pint Test'

Before any piece of copy is published, apply this test:

> *"Would this sound insane if someone said it to you in a pub?"*

If yes — rewrite it.

**Passes the test:**
- "I help businesses show up when their customers are asking AI for recommendations."
- "Search has changed more in the last 18 months than in the previous decade."
- "I'll tell you when AI isn't the right tool for your situation."

**Fails the test:**
- "We leverage generative engine optimisation to maximise AI-driven discovery surfaces."
- "Future-proof your digital presence with cutting-edge AI-native strategies."
- "Transform your business through intelligent content engineering."

The voice is: **informed, direct, Irish, and honest.** It has opinions. It uses plain words. It never oversells.

## The 'Sequential Revelation' Principle

The positioning tension (expertise vs. humility) resolves when **expertise is demonstrated through restraint, not display.**

The site should first establish warmth, specificity, and practicality — then demonstrate depth and expertise.

Think of a highly respected local GP who also happens to be a published researcher. They lead with *"what's the problem, let's figure this out together"* — not *"I have seventeen publications."* The publications matter. They just come second.

**This sequence should be observable in every user journey:**

1. Entry point → feels like talking to someone who understands your specific situation
2. Middle of journey → discovers the depth of knowledge behind the accessible surface
3. Point of contact → feels like a natural continuation of the relationship, not the start of a sales process

## The About Page Imperative

For solo consultants in relationship-first markets, the About page is frequently the **highest-converting page on the site.**

It must be treated with the same strategic intent as any service page. It is not a biography. It is not a CV. It is an argument for why this specific person is the right choice for this specific buyer at this specific moment.

**The About page must answer:**
- Why this person, not an agency?
- What makes this expertise genuine rather than performative?
- What is the moment that made this consultant understand that AI was changing search — told plainly, not heroically?
- What does working with this person actually feel like?

**The About page must not:**
- Lead with credentials
- Use third-person language
- Feel like a LinkedIn profile printed to screen
- Be shorter than 400 words

## Key Copy Principles Summary

| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Specificity over claims** | Replace "many businesses" with a real number, sector, or named outcome |
| **Outcome before service name** | Lead with what changes for the client, bracket with the service term |
| **Questions before answers** | Every FAQ must use the exact language a persona would actually use |
| **Restraint signals expertise** | Saying "this won't work for you" earns more trust than any testimonial |
| **Irish first, global always** | Local cultural register throughout; global credibility signals in the IA layer |

## What Success Looks Like

A CMO in Sandyford or a business owner in Cork should be able to:

1. Find the site via an AI search result (proving the service works)
2. Read the homepage and know immediately: *"This is for someone like me"*
3. Read one Insights article and think: *"This person understands my actual problem"*
4. Read the About page and think: *"I'd be comfortable having a conversation with this person"*
5. Send an email or book a call without feeling like they've entered a sales funnel

If all five of those things happen, the site is working.
